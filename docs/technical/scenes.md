## Reading it (hypothesis)

- The five codes with 300 to 450 nodes each (`ako`, `dea`, `nka`, `sir`, `ski`) look like the five main storylines; `fre` holds the developers' test nodes and shared scenes (`n0fre___*`), and the codes ending in `x` (`nkx`, `skx`) and `soe`/`sox` look like extras and epilogues. This is read from the counts and from the first lines of the nodes, not from a document.
- Kinds with the most nodes: `ev` (669, the story events), `ec` (385) and `me` (366). `kd` appears only with `nka` and `ski`, `lu` and `gd` with several codes.
- The scenario scripts that are not nodes (357) are cut-ins (`c##_cutin`, 35), mahjong classes and events that another event chains to; see `docs/technical/port.md`, "Scripts that are not nodes: chains".

## What running them showed (2026-10-16)

`tools/sweep.py` runs the 2,136 nodes that `NodeTable` lists and that have a script (the inventory above counts 2,129 because its pattern does not match the developers' `n0fre___*` names). Isolated, each in its own process: 2,084 run to their end, 37 fail because they need a game in progress or are developer leftovers, 7 hang or are the developers' interactive nodes (details and causes in `docs/technical/port.md`, "Why scenes fail alone"). The chain sweep (`--chain`) is the second measurement and also reaches the scripts that are not nodes.
